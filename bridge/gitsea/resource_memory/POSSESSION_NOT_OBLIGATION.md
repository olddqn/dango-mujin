# Possession is not obligation.

Dan-Go / GITSEA Bridge · Phase 51 · authority: none · advisory only

---

## The Principle

> **Possession is not obligation.**

Holding a resource does not create a duty to give it away. The state of
having and the state of owing are different things, and Dan-Go keeps
them separate by design.

A companion principle, equally load-bearing:

> **Availability is not transfer.**

Showing that a resource is available does not move it. Making a resource
*legible* and moving its ownership are two different acts, and Dan-Go
performs only the first.

---

## Why This Matters

If possession created obligation:

- The most well-stocked commons would carry the most coerced burden
- "From each according to what they have" would become enforcement
- Resource records would become inventory under public claim
- Generosity given freely would degrade into extraction of the stocked
- Communities would learn to *hide what they hold* to remain free

If availability transferred ownership:

- The first observer would become the new owner
- Documenting a kitchen, a room, a tool would lose the holder their
  authority over it
- The act of legibility would itself be expropriation

The whole purpose of a resource record — making mutual presence legible
without coercion — depends on keeping both of these principles.

---

## The Distinction

| Concept | Source | Dan-Go's stance |
|---|---|---|
| Possession | observed | recorded (Phase 51) |
| Offer | voluntary speech act | recorded (Phase 17) |
| Allocation | external act | **never performed by Dan-Go** |
| Transfer | external act, by the holder, voluntarily | **never performed by Dan-Go** |

Dan-Go can record what a commons holds. Dan-Go can record an offer when
freely made. Dan-Go never converts the first into the second. Dan-Go
never moves a resource. Dan-Go never reassigns ownership.

---

## In Practice

A holder whose resource is recorded should be able to say, without guilt
and without contradiction:

> "I have it. I am keeping it, this time."

And later — if and when they choose — they can say:

> "I have it. I am offering it."

That second statement is **a new speech act** the holder makes, not an
inference Dan-Go draws from the first record. The gap between the two
is the freedom this principle protects.
