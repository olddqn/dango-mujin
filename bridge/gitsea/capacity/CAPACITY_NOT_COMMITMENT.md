# Capacity is not commitment.

Dan-Go / GITSEA Bridge · Phase 50 · authority: none · advisory only

---

## The Principle

> **Capacity is not commitment.**

Recording that a commons *can* do something is not a record that it
*will* do something. A community may have capacity and still choose not
to act — and that choice is fully legitimate.

---

## Why This Matters

If capacity records were read as commitments:

- A commons would become accountable for ability it never pledged
- Observed competence would become a trap (the more able you appear,
  the more you are presumed to owe)
- Communities would learn to **hide capacity** to avoid being drafted
- The act of recording ability would itself become coercive

The whole value of a capacity record — making mutual help legible —
collapses the moment it is mistaken for a promise.

---

## What a Capacity Record Is

A capacity record is an **observation**, in the past tense:

> "This commons has been observed to be able to prepare meals."

It is not:

- A schedule
- A pledge
- A roster
- A duty
- A standing offer

The `availability` field describes an **observed rhythm**
(`weekly`, `on_request`, ...), not a guaranteed availability window.

---

## The Invariant

```yaml
capacity_is_commitment: false
```

This field is present on every capacity record, every snapshot, every
memory, and every report produced by Phase 50. It is permanent and
never flips to `true`.

---

## In Practice

A commons reviewing its own capacity record should be able to say,
without contradiction:

> "Yes, we can do this. No, we are not doing it this week."

Both halves are true at once. Capacity is the first half. Commitment —
if it ever comes — is a separate, voluntary, human act that Dan-Go does
not record as automatic and never infers from capacity.

---

> Capacity is not commitment.
> Ability is not obligation.
> Availability is not allocation.
