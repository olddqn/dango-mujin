# Ability is not obligation.

Dan-Go / GITSEA Bridge · Phase 50 · authority: none · advisory only

---

## The Principle

> **Ability is not obligation.**

Being able to help does not create a duty to help. The capacity to act
and the obligation to act are different things, and Dan-Go keeps them
separate by design.

---

## Why This Matters

If ability created obligation:

- The most capable would carry the most coerced burden
- "From each according to their ability" would become enforcement,
  not solidarity
- Mutual aid would degrade into extraction of the competent
- Demonstrating skill would become dangerous

Solidarity given freely and obligation imposed by record are opposites.
Phase 50 records ability precisely so that it can be offered freely —
never extracted.

---

## The Distinction

| Concept | Source | Dan-Go's stance |
|---|---|---|
| Ability | observed | recorded (Phase 50) |
| Offer | voluntary speech act | recorded (Phase 17) |
| Obligation | — | **never created by Dan-Go** |

Dan-Go can observe ability. Dan-Go can record an offer when freely made.
Dan-Go never converts the first into the second, and never manufactures
obligation from either.

---

## The Invariant

```yaml
ability_creates_obligation: false
```

Present on every Phase 50 record. Permanent. Never flips.

The capacity memory builder additionally carries:

```yaml
memory_matches_capacity_to_need: false
memory_assigns_helpers: false
```

Even when a capacity sits right next to a matching need in the same
memory record, Dan-Go does **not** join them. The juxtaposition is for
human eyes; the decision is for human will.

---

## In Practice

A participant whose capacity is recorded should be able to say, without
guilt and without contradiction:

> "I can. I am choosing not to, this time."

That sentence must remain fully available. If a capacity record ever
made it harder to say, the record would have become an instrument of
obligation — exactly what this principle forbids.
