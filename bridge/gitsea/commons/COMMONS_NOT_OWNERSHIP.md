# Commons Is Not Ownership — Dan-Go Protocol Note

> **"Commons is not ownership."**
> **"Community is not authority."**
> **"Participation is not control."**

## The Distinction

A **commons** is a shared resource or space governed by collective
participation rather than individual ownership. It is held in common —
no single participant owns it, and no single participant can exclude
others by asserting ownership.

**Ownership** is the right to control, alienate, and exclude. Dan-Go
explicitly does not assign ownership of commons to any party, including
itself. The `ownership: false` invariant on every commons record reflects
this unconditionally.

## Why Participation Does Not Confer Ownership

When a participant joins Jammy House, D.R.A., or YacypherPunks — and
that membership is recorded in Dan-Go — the record does not create
a property right. The participant participated. The record reflects that.
No ownership follows.

This matters because:

1. **Ownership would undermine the commons**: If participating in a commons
   created a claim of ownership over it, the commons would dissolve into
   individual property claims. The commons is defined by the absence of
   that dynamic.

2. **Records are advisory, not deeds**: A Dan-Go membership record is an
   advisory observation, not a title document. Treating advisory records
   as ownership claims would corrupt their purpose.

3. **Departure does not transfer**: If a participant leaves a commons, they
   do not take a share of the commons with them, and no ownership record
   in Dan-Go implies otherwise.

## Why Dan-Go Does Not Own the Communities It Records

Dan-Go records Jammy House, YacypherPunks, D.R.A., and other communities.
This creates a responsibility for neutral, accurate record-keeping. It does
not create ownership of those communities.

Dan-Go does not:
- Control how communities operate
- Dictate community membership criteria
- Claim intellectual or organisational ownership of recorded communities
- Represent communities in external negotiations without their consent
- Profit from the existence of recorded communities

Dan-Go observes and records. The community governs itself.

## The Cooperative Basis of the Commons

A cooperative commons (like a housing cooperative, a mutual aid network,
or an open-source project) operates on the principle that participants
share both the benefits and the responsibilities of the shared resource
without individual ownership claims. This is structurally different from
both private ownership and public governance.

Dan-Go's role in cooperative commons is to:
1. Record that the commons exists
2. Record who participates and in what role
3. Record cooperation activities and their outcomes
4. Maintain those records as advisory, append-only, and contestable

It is not Dan-Go's role to adjudicate ownership disputes, assign shares,
or represent any commons in any process it did not initiate through its
own participants.

## Why Append-Only Records Reinforce Non-Ownership

The append-only constraint on commons records means:
- No record can be deleted
- No record can be retroactively rewritten
- New records add to the history without replacing it

This is consistent with the commons model: the history of participation
is shared and permanent, but it is not property. A participant cannot
erase their participation from the record (permanence), and they cannot
claim the record as their property (non-ownership). Both follow from the
same design.

## Protocol Phrase

> "Commons is not ownership."

This phrase appears in every Phase 16 runtime module. It is a statement
of what Dan-Go records and what it does not claim. A commons record is
an advisory observation about shared cooperation — not a deed, not a
title, not a governance instrument. The commons is held in common. The
record is held in common too.
