# Community Is Not Authority — Dan-Go Protocol Note

> **"Community is not authority."**
> **"Commons is not ownership."**
> **"Participation is not control."**

## The Distinction

**Community** is the observable fact of people cooperating over time — sharing
space, interest, infrastructure, or purpose. It is a historical and social
fact. Communities like Jammy House, YacypherPunks, and D.R.A. exist because
people chose to cooperate within them.

**Authority** is the power to compel outcomes — to require action, override
decision, or enforce compliance. Dan-Go has no authority. A community
recorded in Dan-Go has no authority derived from that record. The existence
of a community does not itself generate authority over its members or over
external systems.

## Why Communities Do Not Generate Authority Automatically

Several confusions can make community seem like authority:

1. **Coordination looks like authority**: When a community coordinates action,
   it may appear that someone is in authority. But coordination is voluntary
   agreement, not compulsion. Dan-Go records coordination facts; it does not
   derive authority claims from them.

2. **Shared records look like governance**: Dan-Go maintains a commons
   registry. This might be mistaken for a governance registry. It is not.
   Being in the registry means Dan-Go has observed and recorded the
   community's existence — not that Dan-Go governs it or speaks for it.

3. **History looks like legitimacy**: A long history of cooperation is
   valuable. But historical cooperation does not automatically translate into
   authority over future actors. Jammy House's history in the Dan-Go ledger
   makes its cooperation legible; it does not grant Jammy House authority
   over housing-007 outcomes.

## What Community Records Accomplish (Without Authority)

A community record in Dan-Go:

1. **Makes cooperation legible**: Observers can see that Jammy House
   participated in housing-007, that D.R.A. participated in tenant advocacy
   claims, that YacypherPunks maintains a cooperative digital infrastructure.
   These are observable facts with historical value.

2. **Provides context for contribution records**: Individual contribution
   records in Phases 11–15 gain additional context when the commons
   within which they occurred is also recorded. The contribution history
   becomes a community history.

3. **Supports future commons**: If a Phase 17 or later process wants to
   build on cooperation patterns across communities, the commons registry
   provides a neutral starting record.

4. **Enables voluntary cross-commons coordination**: Communities can observe
   each other's participation in Dan-Go without Dan-Go imposing coordination
   on them.

## The `authority: none` Invariant in Commons Records

Every commons registry entry carries `authority: none`. This is not a
disclaimer added for legal caution — it is a direct statement of the
protocol's design. Dan-Go is an advisory system. Advisory systems have
no authority by definition.

The community existing is a fact.
The community being recorded is advisory.
The advisory record carries no authority.
This chain is unbroken.

## Connection to Jammy House, D.R.A., and Future Commons

Jammy House is a cooperative housing initiative. D.R.A. is a tenant
advocacy initiative. YacypherPunks is a cypherpunk community. Dan-Go is
itself a cooperative project. All of these are commons in the sense that
they operate through voluntary cooperation without individual ownership
of the shared structure.

Dan-Go records all of them equally. None of them receive authority through
that record. All of them remain self-governing.

Future commons — housing cooperatives, mutual aid networks, open-source
collectives — can be registered in the same way. The protocol does not
favour any community over another. Authority remains none for all.

## Protocol Phrase

> "Community is not authority."

This phrase appears in every Phase 16 runtime module. It is the primary
boundary between observable community cooperation and governance claims.
Dan-Go makes communities legible; it does not make them authoritative.
