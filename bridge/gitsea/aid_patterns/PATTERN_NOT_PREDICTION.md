# Pattern Is Not Prediction — Dan-Go Protocol Note

> **"Pattern is not prediction."**
> **"Learning is not prescription."**
> **"Recurrence is not ranking."**

## The Distinction

**A pattern** is an observation about the past. Dan-Go records that a
type of care need has appeared more than once in a particular commons.
The pattern record describes what has been observed across prior care
loops, relief cases, and aid routes. It does not extend forward in time.

**A prediction** is a claim about the future — that a need will recur,
that a specific person will need help again, that a community will face
the same situation. Dan-Go makes no such claims. The `pattern_is_prediction: false`
invariant on every pattern record is unconditional and permanent.

## Why the Distinction Matters

If pattern records were understood as predictions, three harmful
dynamics would follow:

1. **Pre-emptive action without expressed need.** Communities might
   respond to anticipated future need rather than expressed current need.
   This removes the agency of the person in need from the care exchange.
   Dan-Go preserves that agency by recording only what has occurred, not
   what may occur.

2. **Predictive profiling of individuals or communities.** A commons with
   a recurring food support pattern would become associated with food
   insecurity as a defining characteristic. Pattern records would become
   identity records. This is the opposite of what an advisory observation
   layer should do.

3. **Obligation created by prediction.** If a pattern predicts future need,
   participants who saw the prediction might feel responsible to respond
   before the need is expressed. This creates the same obligation structure
   that Phase 17–19 explicitly refused. A prediction that creates obligation
   is a command with extra steps.

Dan-Go avoids all of these by maintaining the strict boundary: a pattern
is what happened. A prediction is what will happen. Dan-Go records the
former. Dan-Go cannot record the latter.

## What the Pattern Record Accomplishes (Without Predicting)

1. **Makes care history legible.** The community can see that a type of
   need has appeared in this commons before. If a new instance of the need
   arises, participants have context — not instructions.

2. **Supports voluntary coordination.** Participants who want to coordinate
   recurring responses may use the pattern information to do so. Participants
   who do not want to do so are not compelled. The pattern record is
   information, not a call to action.

3. **Preserves the append-only record.** Each recurrence observation is
   added to the pattern record. The original care loop records are not
   modified. History grows with the situation.

## The Limit of Pattern Observation

Dan-Go observes patterns. Dan-Go does not:
- Forecast when the next instance will occur
- Identify which specific participant will be in need
- Suggest that the pattern represents a systemic failure
- Recommend any specific response
- Alert any participant that action is required

The pattern ends at the boundary of observation. Everything beyond that
boundary is the community's to determine.

## Protocol Phrase

> "Pattern is not prediction."

This phrase appears in every Phase 20 runtime module. It is the boundary
between Dan-Go's advisory record and any claim about what comes next.
What came before is legible. What comes next is the community's to decide.
